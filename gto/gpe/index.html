A GTO Task

GPE: Group Planning Exercise

Also called the Military Planning Exercise. The GTO shows a map with a story, and several emergencies strike at once. You get about ten minutes to write your own plan before the group thrashes out a common one. It is a test of clear thinking under pressure, not heroics.

How it runs

  • The GTO uncovers a model or map and explains the roads, river, railway, distances and resources.
  • They read a story with several simultaneous problems, then take the story cards away, so you plan from memory (the model stays visible).
  • Each candidate writes an individual plan (about 10 minutes). This is the part you train here.
  • Then a group discussion (about 15 to 20 minutes) to agree one common plan, and a nominee narrates it on the model.
The One Rule

Solve every problem, save human life before anything else, and delegate. A calm plan where named parties act in parallel beats one hero trying to do it all.

Write the plan in this order

  1. 1List every problemNote all of them first. Missing even one problem is a major fault, no matter how good the rest is.
  2. 2List your resourcesMembers, vehicle, boat, cash, rope, and the fixed help on the map: police, hospital, telephone. Use only what you are given, never invent a phone or a chopper.
  3. 3Prioritise, and say whyHuman life in immediate danger first, then a threat to many lives or security, then a single life with some time, then property, then the trivial. The reasoning matters more than the exact order.
  4. 4Divide into partiesSplit the group into Party A, B, C with a leader each, tackling problems in parallel. Match the fastest resource to the most urgent, life-threatening problem.
  5. 5Make each action time-boundUse the stated distances and speeds. Keep it physically possible: 20 km by cycle is not five minutes. Account for travel, action and return.
  6. 6RegroupState where and when the group reconvenes, so the plan closes cleanly.

The priority ladder

When two problems compete, this is the order assessors expect, with your reasoning attached:

  • Human life in immediate danger (fire trapping people, drowning, serious injury).
  • Threat to many lives or national security (warning an oncoming train, a bomb, infiltration).
  • A single life with a little time (a stable patient, blood needed within the hour).
  • Property or financial loss (a robbery, a theft).
  • The trivial problem last.

Saving the injured comes before catching thieves. Say so, and act on it.

Good versus weak, in practice

Weak. "I will rush to the accident, take the injured to hospital, then come back, catch the dacoits, put out the fire and rescue the child. I will manage everything." One person, everything in sequence, no timings, ignores what is most urgent.

Stronger. "Party A (2 members) to the burning house at once to rescue the child with the villagers. Party B (2 members, jeep) load the injured, alert the hospital by the PCO on the way, 10 km in about 15 minutes. Party C (1 member) to the police post, 6 km, to report the dacoity. Regroup at the village square by 0700." All problems, life before property, parallel, named parties, time-bound, real resources.

What the GTO is looking for

GPE mainly shows your effective intelligence, reasoning and organising ability: do you grasp the whole situation, prioritise soundly, allocate people and resources well, and keep it practical and time-bound. Clear, structured writing is itself assessed, and in the discussion, cooperation and helping the group agree count more than winning.

What sinks a plan

  • Missing a problem, or planning before listing them all.
  • Prioritising property or thieves above injured people.
  • Doing everything yourself, with no delegation.
  • Ignoring time and distance, or impossible timings.
  • Inventing resources not in the story, or filmi, superhuman solutions.
  • Splitting the group illogically, or leaving actions vague with no who and when.
  • Contradicting the stated facts, distances or time of day.

The group discussion and narration

After the individual plans, the group discusses to build one common plan, and a nominee narrates it on the model. Contribute your priority order and reasoning early, stay open to better ideas, and help the group reach consensus. Do not dominate, argue or go silent. If you narrate, keep it logical: the main problems, the resources, the party-wise tasks with timings, and the outcome.
