The Screening Test

PPDT: Picture Perception & Description Test

Day one, the gate. One hazy picture, thirty seconds to look, then you note what you saw and write a short hero story. Narrate it, discuss it in a group, and the board decides: screened in, or on the bus home.

What it really tests

PPDT is a filter. Only screened-in candidates stay for the four days that follow, so this one picture and story carry a lot of weight. It checks how you read an ambiguous, dim scene under time pressure, whether your instinct is positive and constructive, and whether you can build a clear story around a hero who acts.

It comes in two halves: the written perception and story, then narration and a group discussion where the batch builds one common story. This page trains the written half. The discussion half is covered at the end.

The One Rule

See it positively, write one clear hero who takes action, and make sure the story matches the characters you noted. Coherence and a constructive outcome are everything.

The format, in short

  • One hazy picture, shown for about 30 seconds, then removed.
  • About a minute to note your perception on the sheet.
  • About four minutes to write the story.
  • Then narration (about a minute each) and a group discussion to reach a common story.

The perception step

This is what sets PPDT apart from TAT. Before the story, you record what you saw:

  1. 1Number of charactersHow many people are in the scene. Get this right; a careless count that your story then contradicts is an easy red flag.
  2. 2Age, sex and mood of the main characterNote the hero's age as a number, sex as M, F or P (person, if unclear), and mood as positive, negative or neutral. Lean positive.
  3. 3Mark one heroPick a single main character, usually close to your own age and sex, so the story is relatable and your projection shows.
  4. 4The action, in a few wordsThe central constructive activity, for example "organising flood relief" or "counselling a junior". This is the seed of your story, not a description of the scenery.

The story

Write a complete little story around that one hero, in about 80 to 100 words, in a consistent tense. Keep the three beats:

  • Cause: what led to the moment, kept brief and plausible.
  • Action: what the hero decides and does, using real, available resources. This is where character shows.
  • Outcome: a positive, believable result that the hero's action earned.

The hero should be the main character you noted, and the people in the story should match your perceived count. Give the hero a name and a role, and let them act rather than wait.

Good versus weak, in practice

The perception line.
Weak: "Some people, looks sad, not sure."
Stronger: "3 characters. Hero: male, 25, positive. Action: youth organising a village clean-up."

The story.
Weak: "A girl was weak in studies, her mother asked the teacher for help, and later she improved."
Stronger: "Arjun, 24, noticed a junior falling behind. He drew up a simple study plan, arranged senior guidance and daily practice, and kept encouraging her. Within weeks her marks climbed, and she thanked him for believing in her."

What gets candidates screened out

  • Negative, violent or tragic themes, or a story that ends in failure.
  • A story whose characters or hero do not match the perception you noted.
  • A group story with no single hero, or a passive hero who waits to be rescued.
  • Unrealistic or superhuman heroics.
  • Just describing the picture instead of telling a story with cause, action and outcome.
  • An incomplete story, or running out of time.

The group discussion that follows

After writing, you narrate your story to the group in about a minute, then the batch discusses to build one common story. Narrate confidently with steady pace and eye contact on the group, not the assessors. In the discussion, contribute real points, listen, find the common threads, and help the group converge. Do not dominate, do not go silent, and never argue aggressively or shout others down. Being unable to narrate or discuss is how strong writers still get screened out.
